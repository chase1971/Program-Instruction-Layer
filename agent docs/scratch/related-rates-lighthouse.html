Related rates — lighthouse problem

A lighthouse sits on an island 3 km from the nearest point P on a straight shoreline, centered in the frame, and its beam sweeps at a constant angular rate all the way from one side of P to the other. The point where the beam hits the shore does not move at a constant rate, though — it's x = 3×tan(θ), negative to the left of P and positive to the right — so the point crawls slowly as it passes under the lighthouse and accelerates hard toward either end as the beam nears parallel to the shore. Marks x = 1 km, the distance the textbook question asks about. The real 4 rev/min sweep (8π/60 ≈ 0.42 rad/sec) would cover this whole range in a couple of seconds — too fast to watch the crawl-then-blur happen at both ends — so it is slowed to roughly a third of real speed here; the 3 km distance and the tangent relationship stay exact.
