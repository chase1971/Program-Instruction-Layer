Guildrun Beginner Tier & Item Reference

Printable guide • researched August 30, 2026 • Demo-era rankings change frequently with patches

How to use this: Tier does not mean “always buy.” Rank, specialization, items, boss matchup, and whether the hero fills your missing role can matter more. For a new player, first secure one durable frontline + one real damage dealer; use the third field slot and backups for support and synergy.

What the letters hide

Excellent general pickups

  • Grace: unusually flexible support; Selfless is a strong Backup shield option.
  • Sal: Frost is valuable for both survival and damage support.
  • Funke: reliable hallway/AoE carry; current Burn scaling rewards Magic.
  • Pimenta / Rowan: flexible frontlines that connect with several class plans.

Strong, but needs a condition

  • Irini / Nyx / Rip: much stronger with Rush, Stall, Duelist, or Attack Speed engines.
  • Karsu: valuable boss/Frost solution, less reliable in ordinary hallway fights.
  • Ratna: much better when acquired and scaled early, or found already ranked.
  • Pollen / Gustav: need time and mana/support conditions to show their value.

High-level players disagree with the published ordering. Aesah’s patch-0.5.2 guide rated Funke first and Tilly second, emphasized B-rank tank by the first boss and A-rank by the last boss, and treated Karsu/Irini/Sal/Nyx as important boss-scaling options. That disagreement is a reminder to rank heroes by the job your run needs.

Beginner item priorities (more useful than a universal item tier list)

No reliable, current written source ranks every item. Item value is too dependent on holder, trigger, and matchup. Use these priority bands while learning.

PriorityWhat to buyExamples / reason
HighImmediate frontline durabilityDefense plus HP, shields, or HP/s. Even a plain Bulwark (+30 Defense) can be a major early upgrade. Tank items buy time for every carry.
HighCorrect primary stat for your carryAttack/Attack Speed/Crit for auto attackers; Magic and appropriate mana stats for spell carries. Read the exact ability formula first.
HighStarting Mana when first cast is lateOften useful even late. It can move a fight-winning heal, shield, or nuke ahead of enemy burst.
HighFrost application or amplificationFrost lowers enemy Defense and Attack Speed, supporting both offense and survival.
SituationalOmnivampExcellent when a carry survives long enough to deal large, repeated damage. Weak if the holder dies before acting.
SituationalBurn/Poison amplifiersExcellent only after your team reliably applies meaningful stacks. Do not buy the amplifier before the engine exists.
SituationalRush/Stall/Shard trigger itemsPowerful when the trigger occurs repeatedly. Count likely triggers; do not value the fantasy version of the item.
Usually skipWrong-stat or unreliable-trigger itemsMagic on a hero whose useful effects do not scale with Magic; On-Crit effects with very low Crit; Stall effects when fights end before activation.

Ten-second shop test

  1. Name the recipient. If you cannot, the purchase is probably not urgent.
  2. Check the base stats. Are they useful even if the special effect fails?
  3. Check the trigger. Will your current team activate it repeatedly?
  4. Check the problem. Does it prevent frontline collapse, speed a key cast, or raise inadequate damage?
  5. Check the slot. Is it better than the weakest item already on that hero?

Simple allocation plan

Main tank

Give the first strong defensive items here. Mix Defense with HP/shields/healing rather than chasing elaborate effects.

Main carry

Concentrate matching offense here. Add Omnivamp if damage is sustained; add HP/Defense/shields if the carry dies before dealing damage.

Third field hero

Usually receives leftovers unless one item enables a critical heal, shield, Frost application, or cast timing.

Backups

Buy for the Backup effect and class contribution. Do not spend heavily itemizing a hero who will not enter combat.
